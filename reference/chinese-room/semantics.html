01
A closer look

Syntax and semantics are distinct

A formal rule specifies how marks are manipulated without fixing what they refer to. Searle takes that distinction to show that the formal program alone is insufficient for intrinsic meaning. But the conceptual difference between two properties doesn't establish that one could never emerge from a particular organization of the other. Searle 1990, p. 21 ↗

02
A closer look

Integrated cognitive architecture

Aaron Sloman and Monica Croucher suggest that understanding may involve a system of interacting representations, goals, beliefs, perception, and reasoning. The proposed mechanism is not an isolated word-to-word mapping; it is a network that uses representations in a wider cognitive economy. Sloman & Croucher, p. 447 ↗

03
A closer look

Searle's response

Searle replies that enlarging the program does not escape his internalization scenario; the operator can still execute the entire extended program. His objection is that formal relations still do not supply intrinsic semantic content. The open question is whether those integrated relations could constitute it. Searle's response, p. 454 ↗

04
A closer look

Haugeland's semantic activity

John Haugeland develops a richer candidate: internal inferences and world-involving transactions maintain a fit between representation and environment. Searle answers that this might still be an observer-relative attribution of meaning; he contrasts a complex automobile engine with an infant. Haugeland, p. 432 ↗ Searle's response, p. 453 ↗

05
A closer look

A continuing research program

Paul Thagard's 1986 paper addresses the emergence of meaning explicitly, while William Rapaport develops conceptual-role and syntactic semantics. These are later positions, not verified next turns in a historical Searle dialogue. They take seriously the possibility that meaning is an organizational achievement, while facing the separate problem of unique external reference. Rapaport's papers ↗

Return to the main question

The central debate is not decided by a metaphor or by defining a symbol as meaningless. The disputed issue is which properties of a complete physical system are necessary and sufficient for semantic understanding.
