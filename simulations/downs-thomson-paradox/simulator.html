The experiment

Does a better road create a better equilibrium?

First let the old city settle. Then open the tunnel and let the same commuters respond to the new network.
Realism review: what is modeled, and what is still simplified?

Physical units are separate from drawing units. Road links have modeled distances and speeds; the map is only a visualization. Cars therefore no longer become less capable merely because they are drawn large on the screen.

Signals have explicit phases and queue discharge. Green time, yellow/all-red clearance, roughly two seconds of startup lost time, and a configurable per-lane saturation flow govern how quickly a standing queue can leave. Multiple queued vehicles can pass during one green. Each lane has its own queue and discharge clock.

Driving is still microscopic. Individual cars accelerate, follow leaders with a time headway, stop at red lights, wait for downstream space, change road links, and can spill queues backward. Roads are bidirectional and the arterial/tunnel can have multiple lanes per direction.

The surrounding city now routes traffic. Ambient vehicles are generated from multiple origins and destinations and find shortest free-flow paths through local streets, north/south cross streets, a southern parallel road, the mountain route, and — after construction — the tunnel. Some background traffic therefore reroutes when the tunnel opens too.

Still simplified: there are no pedestrians, bicycles, buses in mixed traffic, crashes, parking maneuvers, lane-changing incentives, protected/permissive left-turn phases, stochastic signal actuation, or detailed intersection conflict geometry. Turns use the signal phase of their approach. Those are good candidates for later complexity if they help the lesson rather than bury it.
