What the model assumes

  • Families occupy adjacent seats and enter as one contiguous boarding unit. Their colors and labels distinguish one party from the next.
  • Party-size inputs are relative weights. They are normalized to 100% at run time, so totals above or below 100 work normally; all-zero weights use an equal split.
  • Load factor controls occupied seats; unassigned seats remain visibly empty.
  • Sequence compliance is applied to whole parties within their normal priority tier.
  • Children walk and sit more slowly; assisted travelers board with a companion.
  • Carry-ons block the aisle; occupied aisle/middle seats create seat interference.
  • The same manifest, traits, and compliance draws are reused for all six methods.

Method and model details

Random: shuffles boarding units. Strict back/front: orders rows exactly, randomizing only within a row. Airline zones: uses six-row rear-first blocks. WilMA: boards window, then middle, then aisle priorities. Group-safe Steffen: ranks window seats first and alternates row parity and side. For WilMA and Steffen, each party enters at its earliest member's rank.

Seat conflicts: when a passenger reaches a seat, the simulator counts each already-seated passenger between the aisle and that seat on the same side of the row. Each same-party blocker adds 2.2 seconds to seating time; a blocker from another party adds 5.4 seconds. Thus a window passenger arriving after both the aisle and middle passengers adds two conflicts.

Blocked aisle: cumulative simulated wall-clock time during which at least one passenger is stowing a bag or entering a seat. Multiple simultaneous blockers are counted once for that time interval, and ordinary walking queues do not add to this metric.

Visible luggage: passengers with carry-ons keep a suitcase marker while walking. During stowing it moves into the overhead strip on the correct row side, then remains visible there. This visualizes the existing luggage timing model; bins still have no hard capacity or overflow search.

This is a comparative discrete-time model, not a calibrated airline forecast. The useful claim is the relative result under stated assumptions; the absolute minutes are scenario output, not revealed scripture.
